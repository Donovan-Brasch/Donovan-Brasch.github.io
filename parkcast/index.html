    How this works

    I made this because getting a campsite at a popular park comes down to knowing exactly when booking opens and how fast it goes, and that info is either buried or nowhere. So I pulled it all into one place.

    When booking opens comes from each campground's posted rule (like "six months ahead at 7 am"). No posted rule? I use when bookings started last year.

    How fast it fills comes from every Recreation.gov reservation from 2022 to 2025. The forecast is basically 2025's pattern for the same month and type of night, moved onto this year's booking dates. I tried averaging all three years and adjusting for park visitor trends, and both did worse, so I kept it simple.

    Cancellations: if the same site gets booked twice for one night, the first person cancelled. That's how I count reopened sites.

    How accurate? To check, I pretended it was 2023 and used that year's data to forecast 2025 (the same two-year gap as forecasting 2027 from 2025 now), then compared against what really happened. Fill timing was usually off by about 25%: if I said a campground would be half full 40 days after opening, the real answer landed around 30 to 50. Sell-out chances were usually within about 15 points, which is why "Fills up?" shows a range.

    Park info: visitors per day are a forecast built from the Park Service's monthly counts going back to 1979, and overnight stays use each month's recent share of visitors who stay in lodges, campgrounds or the backcountry. Weather is 10 to 15 years of daily history from Open-Meteo. Visitors per mile of road uses the Federal Highway Administration's count of paved public roads in each park, which gives a feel for how packed the roads get.
